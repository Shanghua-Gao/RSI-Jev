# RSI-Jev v2.1

*Describes release v2.1 · updated 2026-09-27*

One typed-decision model, 2B. Given a document and a set of typed questions — yes/no,
pick-one-of-*k*, rate-on-a-rubric — it returns a calibrated probability for every option
in **one forward pass**, with no generated text. The task, the architecture and the
confidence head are [v2.0](v2.0.md)'s, unchanged.

[v2.0](v2.0.md) had to be published with a caveat: its suite gain came very largely from
training on the suite's own train splits, and on three benchmarks frozen before the search
began it did not beat [v1.0](v1.0.md). **v2.1 is the version where that caveat goes away.**
It is ahead of v1.0 on all three of those held-out benchmarks, significantly so on two,
and it recovers the general knowledge v2.0 lost.

---

## 1. The task

Unchanged from [v1.0](v1.0.md#1-the-task) and [v2.0](v2.0.md#1-the-task): a document (the
*state*) plus one or more typed questions — `noul`, `choice`, `score` — and a probability
per option, returned in one forward pass. **Pooled top-1** is the fraction of decisions
whose highest-probability option matches the teacher's modal label; **ECE** is expected
calibration error, the average gap between the confidence a model reports and how often it
is right at that confidence, weighted by bin size, lower being better.

## 2. How the model is built and trained

The architecture is v1.0's and the confidence head is v2.0's, both unchanged; see
[v2.0 §2](v2.0.md#2-how-the-model-is-built-and-trained). Three things changed, all in the
data and the optimiser, and none of them in how inference works.

1. **The procedural benchmark's train split** joined the corpus (3k cases). v2.0's corpus
   did not have it, and procedural was one of the two benchmarks v2.0 got *worse* at.
2. **The general-knowledge replay was weighted toward science**, and half of it recast to
   ten options so its shape matches MMLU-Pro's.
3. **The bottom 8 of the tower's 24 layers train at one tenth the learning rate.**

The third is the one worth reading twice. v2.0's single real regression was general
knowledge: MMLU-Pro fell from v1.0's 0.355 to 0.305 while every decision benchmark rose.
Five things were tried against it:

| the repair | MMLU-Pro | what it cost |
|---|---|---|
| a retention KL against the base model's next-token distribution | 0.3085 | nothing, and it did not work |
| more replay, weighted to science, half recast to ten options | 0.3307 | nothing |
| the whole tower at half the learning rate | 0.3273 | nothing, and inside the noise |
| **freeze** the tower's lower third | 0.3390 | three benchmarks regressed |
| **the bottom 8 layers at one tenth the learning rate** | **0.3590** | nothing |

The first is the interesting failure. Holding the tower's *language modelling* close to the
base model works as an objective — the retention KL stayed near 0.02 nats per token — and
MMLU-Pro eroded anyway. What the decision readout uses is not what next-token prediction
preserves, so protecting the latter does not protect the former.

The rest say that this was never a data problem. More and better-shaped replay moves
MMLU-Pro by +0.008. A uniformly smaller learning rate moves it by nothing. Freezing the
lower layers moves it by +0.016 and breaks three decision benchmarks, because those layers
do need to adapt. Letting them adapt **slowly** gets more of the knowledge back than
freezing them does and costs nothing at all: +0.054 on the recipe, and past v1.0. Fitting a
decision objective through the whole tower at one rate was overwriting the representation
the knowledge sat in.

**Reproducing its training run.** Re-scored from disk with this repo's code, the checkpoint
reproduces the training run's per-question predictions at **1.0000** on all three
verification targets — typed-decisions in both option orders, and MMLU-Pro. `verify.json`
is that re-scoring, run on an H100.

**The same check on a second GPU architecture returns 0.9990 on MMLU-Pro** — one question in
a thousand — and that is the more useful fact. `verify_blackwell.json` is that run, shipped
beside the first rather than discarded, because it is the evidence for the ±0.001 below.
Neither figure was chosen over the other; both are what the published code returned on the
machine it ran on.

`verify_bf16.json` is the bf16 tower, `roundtrip_200.json` the reload check with the
calibration applied, `cached_agreement.json` the check that the two serving paths — with and
without the prefix cache — return **identical** probabilities (162 questions, 0 flips,
largest difference 0.0), and `MANIFEST.json` the sha256 of every file so a download can be
checked.

MMLU-Pro on this checkpoint should be read as ±0.001. Three of its thousand questions have
a top-2 logit margin below 0.001 — the narrowest 0.00004 — and eighteen are below 0.01,
against a median of 0.534. (Those margins are a property of the predictions, so
`load_release.py --ckpt DIR --verify` regenerates them and you can recount.) Reordering an fp32 sum across GPU architectures moves a logit by
about that much, so a handful of MMLU-Pro answers are decided by summation order rather than
by the model. [v2.0 §3](v2.0.md#3-checkpoints) works the effect through in full.

The checkpoint ships `calibration.safetensors` and `calibration.json`; `load_release.py`
applies them automatically. Without them the model loads **uncalibrated** and its
probabilities are the raw ones, which match no number in this card.

Apache-2.0, following the base model, as v1.0 and v2.0 are.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 The held-out comparison — read this first

Three benchmarks were frozen before any of this work started, and are read once per
release. Neither v1.0 nor v2.1 trained on them, so this is the only place the two are
measured on equal terms. All three models were scored in **one job, on one code path**.

| held out from the whole search | n | v1.0 | v2.0 | **v2.1** | v2.1 − v1.0 | paired *p* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tasksource | 979 | 0.5771 | 0.5761 | **0.6159** | **+0.0388** | 0.011 |
| SemIf external | 252 | 0.8492 | 0.8492 | **0.8849** | +0.0357 | 0.18 |
| scienthoon OOD | 873 | 0.7148 | 0.6907 | **0.7743** | **+0.0596** | 2.0e−05 |

*p* is an exact two-sided McNemar test on the decisions where the two models disagree.
Against v2.0 rather than v1.0 the same test gives +0.0398 (*p*=0.0033), +0.0357
(*p*=0.078) and +0.0836 (*p*=5.4e−10).

**v2.1 is a better decision model than v1.0, not only a better suite score.** SemIf is
positive but 252 questions give it little power, so read it as consistent with the other
two rather than as evidence on its own.

### 4.3 Calibration

The confidence head is v2.0's, fitted the same way and in the same mode
(`oof_head_scorefloor`); see [v2.0 §2.1](v2.0.md#21-the-confidence-head). Suite ECE is flat
against v2.0 (0.0556 against 0.0546) — v2.1's improvement is accuracy, not calibration.

On typed-decisions' 2,000 test decisions, binned by the confidence v2.1 reported
(`python scripts/calibration.py --model v2.1-2b` prints this table):

| the model says | decisions | actually right |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.9 | 519 | 0.981 |
| 0.7 – 0.9 | 542 | 0.852 |
| 0.5 – 0.7 | 719 | 0.688 |
| < 0.5 | 220 | 0.523 |

Acting on the most confident *x*% of those decisions and deferring the rest: 100% → 0.790,
80% → 0.853, 60% → 0.893, 40% → 0.950, 20% → 0.993 — `scripts/routing.py` is the same
measurement, and [`../BENCHMARKS.md`](../BENCHMARKS.md#reproducing-each-table) says how to
run both.

On the held-out benchmarks calibration travels unevenly. ECE is the best of the three
models on scienthoon (0.0824 → 0.0599 → **0.0542**) and recovers most of v2.0's tasksource
regression (0.0460 → 0.0888 → 0.0614), but on SemIf it is worse than either
(0.0943 → 0.0916 → **0.1193**) — there v2.1 is **under**-confident, reporting a mean 0.770
against 0.885 accuracy, which is the conservative direction but is still error.

## 5. Speed, the wire format, and what `confidence` means

All unchanged from v2.0, which is unchanged from v1.0: one tower pass per question, the
calibration is a division inside the readout, and nothing generates text. **52 ms** to read
the document and **11.5 ms** per decision on an NVIDIA GB10, bf16; reading the document
dominates, so a batch of questions about one document is the shape to ask in. See
[v2.0 §5](v2.0.md#5-speed) and [§7](v2.0.md#7-what-it-costs-to-run); the weights are the
same size and the calibration files add 140 KB.

The API returns a probability *and* a `confidence`, and they are different numbers. The
probability is the model's mass on an option. `confidence` is the peak statistic Jev
documents, `(K * p_max - 1) / (K - 1)` — 0 at uniform, 1 at one-hot, absent from `noul`
answers. Route on the probability of the option you intend to act on, as
[§4.3](#43-calibration) does; reach for `confidence` when what you want to know is whether
the model is spread across options.

## 6. Limitations

- **Its benchmark scores are not zero-shot.** Eleven of the twelve suite benchmarks now
  contribute train-split data — procedural joined the list this release. Do not compare
  those numbers with any model's zero-shot numbers, including v1.0's. The held-out three in
  [§4.1](#41-the-held-out-comparison--read-this-first) are the comparison that is fair.
- **procedural's +0.25 is in-domain.** It is the largest single number in this release and
  the least interesting one.
- **General knowledge is recovered, not proven intact.** 0.383 beats v1.0's 0.355 and
  v2.0's 0.305 on MMLU-Pro, and that is one benchmark of general knowledge measured as a
  typed decision. Fine-tuning a tower for a single-position decision objective destroys its
  language modelling outright — the tuned tower emits whitespace — so "does not forget"
  is a claim about this benchmark and nothing wider.
- **SemIf calibration got worse**, in the under-confident direction. [§4.3](#43-calibration).
- **The corpus gate failed and was overridden.** Adding procedural shifts the corpus's
  label mix away from the reference profile: first-option rate 0.278 against 0.507, and the
  score-level histogram moves (total variation 0.150), because procedural's rubrics have
  more levels than the rest of the corpus. The mix **is** the experiment here, so the gate
  was overridden deliberately and the failure recorded rather than tuned away.
- **The held-out set is spent.** Those three benchmarks have now been read for two
  releases. A held-out set is consumable, so v2.2 needs a freshly frozen one; see
  [`../BENCHMARKS.md`](../BENCHMARKS.md).
- **Reproducing the suite needs upstream checkouts**, and two of its corpus inputs are not
  downloadable yet. Unchanged from [v2.0 §9](v2.0.md#9-limitations).
- **One model, one size, one seed.**

## 7. How it got here

The chain from v2.0 to here, out of the arms run after it:

| arm | headline | what it established |
|---|---|---|
| `mmlu-keep-1a-retention-kl` | MMLU-Pro 0.3085 | keeping the tower's next-token distribution near the base model's does not keep the decision readout's general knowledge |
| `suite-train-2-cap3k-rep15stem10` | MMLU-Pro 0.3307 | science-weighted replay, half of it recast to ten options: +0.008, and nothing on the suite. The best MMLU-Pro of any data arm |
| `mmlu-keep-1b-st-tower-half` | MMLU-Pro 0.3273 | halving the learning rate of the *whole* tower does nothing |
| `mmlu-keep-1c-freeze-lower-third` | MMLU-Pro 0.3390 | freezing the lower third recovers +0.016 but regresses three benchmarks. A trade, not a keeper |
| `cand-2b-1b-freeze4-stem-proc` | MMLU-Pro 0.327, suite 0.7185 | freezing only 4 layers, with the new corpus, is weaker still |
| `cand-2b-1a-lowerlr01-stem-proc` | MMLU-Pro 0.359, suite 0.7258 | the bottom 8 at lr ×0.1 beats freezing them, and costs nothing. **v2.1's recipe** |
| `cand-2b-1a-...-confirm` | suite 0.7286, MMLU-Pro 0.360 | confirmed on a fresh seed, and larger there than on the two it was selected on |
| `rc-B-cand1a-cal4b` | suite 0.7291, ECE 0.0556 | the recipe plus v2.0's confidence head, seed 17. **This checkpoint** |

Rejected along the way, and worth knowing because each closes a direction: a 4B tower on
v2.0's recipe (failed to train at all), the lr ×0.1 treatment at 0.8B (0.6834, discarded —
the 0.8B line's own keeper is a different recipe at 0.6791, well below 2B),
recasting *all* of the replay to ten options rather than half, raising the replay dose to
30%, eleven extra coverage sources, two round-2 data-selection policies head to head (no
difference), and eleven more reinforcement-learning arms — four of them against a matched
supervised control for the first time, and every one of those landed at or below its control.
Counting every arm whose training signal was a reward rather than the teacher's target
distribution, this project has now run **eighteen and kept none**. The full record is
[`../EXPLORE.md`](../EXPLORE.md).

**A rule was changed mid-search**, and this release should be read with it in mind. The
number of seeds required to confirm a candidate was changed from a fixed four to scaling
with the effect size: one seed by default, a gap of about +0.015 or more confirmed by one
fresh seed, smaller gaps by up to three plus a confirmation seed. v2.1's recipe was
confirmed under the new rule — two seeds plus fresh seed 97 — not the old one. The
reasoning and the original rule are in [`../BENCHMARKS.md`](../BENCHMARKS.md).
