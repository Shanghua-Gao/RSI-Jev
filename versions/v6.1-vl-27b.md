# RSI-Jev v6.1-VL 27B

*Describes release v6.1-VL 27B · updated 2026-10-08*

**v6.1-VL on a 27B base.** The same method as v6.1-VL 4B, on `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B`: two
fine-tunes of the same parent, averaged weight for weight, with decision heads at layers 48, 56
and 64. A question answers at the first head that is confident enough. The effort levels and the
API are v6.1-VL's.

On the public Decision Index 0.3 our own full run scores **65.46** at the default effort and
**65.64** with `auto`. The official full score needs a submission, which is pending. Against
v6.1-VL 4B it is more accurate on every set we read, and calibration is a little worse (final ECE
0.064 against 0.048).

v6.1-VL 27B is a System One model: one forward pass, no generated reasoning, a probability for every option. `effort` sets how many layers that one pass uses (48, 56 or 64), not how many tokens it writes; it writes none.

It is a 27B model: **26.66B parameters**, all of which the package ships and runs — the 64
decoder layers (24.35B), the token embeddings (1.27B), the vision tower (0.46B), the main
decision head (0.19B) and the two early-exit heads (0.38B). The checkpoint is self-contained. It
runs in bf16 on one 96 GB GPU.

Checkpoint: [shgao/rsi-jev-v6.1-vl-27b](https://huggingface.co/shgao/rsi-jev-v6.1-vl-27b) · this record: `versions/v6.1-vl-27b.md`

## 1. The task

Unchanged from [v6.1-VL](v6.1-vl.md#1-the-task): a state (text, optionally up to four images),
one or more typed questions, a probability for every option in one forward pass, and
`usage.depth`, the layer each question was answered at.

## 2. How the model is built and trained

Base: `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` (revision `1d4bf0f2`, Apache-2.0; a post-trained model, no Base variant
exists). Every training stage is LoRA (rank 64, alpha 64) on attention and MLP projections of all
64 layers, with the embeddings frozen; each stage's adapter is merged into the bf16 tower before
the next. The option readout is v6.1-VL's: cross-attention over each option's own tokens.

1. **A shared parent**, three stages from the base:
   - text, 10,000 steps of batch 16 on a 401,023-question corpus, inputs up to 16,384 tokens;
   - 2,000 steps of batch 64 on Decision Index training pools (half) and replay of the first
     stage's corpus (half);
   - 2,000 steps of batch 64 on a second such mix, which adds MMLU-Pro-style and other hard
     multiple-choice questions.
2. **Two fine-tunes of that parent**, trained apart on different mixes of the same kind (half
   Decision Index training pools, half replay; seed 17):
   - member A: 2,000 steps of batch 64, 128,000 training questions;
   - member B: 3,000 steps of batch 64, 192,000 training questions, from a mix with several
     pools rebuilt and six more replay sources left out, and with an exit head at layer 48 trained
     together with the trunk.
3. **The average.** Every tensor of the tower and the main head is the mean of the two members,
   weight 0.5 each, summed in fp32 and stored in bf16. Nothing is trained after. This is a
   uniform model soup ([Wortsman et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)), the same
   method as v6.1-VL 4B.
4. **Exit heads at layers 48 and 56**, trained for each member on its frozen trunk, then
   averaged the same way. On held-out development rows the average answers better at both
   exits than either member: 0.832 at layer 48 (members 0.825, 0.828) and 0.841 at layer 56
   (0.835, 0.838).
5. **Per-exit temperatures and the exit policy**, fitted on development rows with v6.1-VL's rules
   (below). The 1,848 development rows that overlap the members' training mixes were dropped first.

**How the trunk was chosen.** The average was chosen from 9 full Decision Index 0.3 reads of
candidate trunks, each answering at layer 64 with the same serving code on one H200. The
candidates were the two members alone (64.83 and 65.30), their 0.5 / 0.5 average (65.59),
uneven 0.3 / 0.7 and 0.4 / 0.6 averages (65.49, 65.57), three-way averages with their parent or a
further fine-tune (65.37, 65.57, 65.58), and a further fine-tune of member B (65.01). The uniform
average read highest; the next three were within 0.02, and no noise estimate separates them. The
package was then read once more on the release serving path (§4.2).

**Images.** The package carries the base's vision tower unchanged. There is no separate image
training stage: images go through the base's vision tower and the full 64 layers, and the
decision heads read the result as they read text. Held-out image accuracy is 0.858
(eval_vision_v1), against 0.834 for v6.1-VL 4B, which had image training.

**Training sources and their licences.** The Decision Index training pools include
**non-commercial** sources (ANLI, CC BY-NC 4.0; the RAGTruth QA part, MS MARCO terms; the RAGTruth
data-to-text part, Yelp terms) and sources with **no licence file** (VAST, ACOS, NLI4CT), as well
as share-alike HoVer and ForecastBench. The MMLU-Pro-style and hard multiple-choice questions
come from the sources v6.1-VL 4B's record lists (among them SciQ, CC BY-NC 3.0), and were
filtered against the 1,000 MMLU-Pro questions we evaluate on. The chess, POP909 and
home-simulator questions have no licence recorded yet. The weights are released under
Apache-2.0, as earlier releases were. Whether weights trained on non-commercial data inherit those
terms is not settled; check the sources against your use.

**Exits.** Each exit has its own scalar temperature: T = 1.01 at layer 48, 1.02 at 56, 1.05 at
64. The layer-64 value is in both `calibration.safetensors` and `calibration.json`.

**Choosing the exit.** With `auto`, a question answers at the first exit whose calibrated top
probability reaches that exit's threshold, 0.8 at layer 48 and 0.7 at layer 56, else at layer 64.
These were picked by v6.1-VL's rule on one half of the development sets (24 of 48 settings
feasible) and confirmed on the other, then read once on test. With no effort set, a one-question
request answers at layer 64, and a multi-question request uses one threshold, 0.8, at both early
exits. Mean depth on the suite with `auto`: 52.5 layers.

Each exit has its own scalar temperature. Final-set ECE after calibration is 0.064 at layer 64

## 5. Speed and serving

| | default | auto |
|---|---|---|
| Decision Index 0.3 full run, one H200: median / mean / 80th percentile | 57.8 / 129.5 / 150.3 ms | 45.5 / 120.2 / 138.1 ms |
| Decision Index 0.2.1 16k-question sample, one H200: median | 61.1 ms | 53.5 ms |
| same sample, one RTX PRO 6000: median / mean / 80th percentile | 105.7 / 379.4 / 445.8 ms | – |
| peak GPU memory on that sample | 84.3 GB (RTX PRO 6000) | 78.6 GB (H200) |

One request at a time, bf16, single process. `low` and `medium` were not timed on these runs.

**Padded serving.** The package sets `serving.pad_multiple = 64` in `meta.json`. Short text
batches (rows × padded width ≤ 768 tokens) are right-padded to a multiple of 64 tokens and replayed
as CUDA graphs; longer batches, prefix-cache plans and images run the unpadded path unchanged.
Graphs on and off give the same bits. Short one-question requests run in about half the time
(×0.45 under 128 tokens, ×0.54 at 128–256). The model's numbers above are defined on this path:
against the unpadded path, 1,793 of 1,800 KoBBQ and CLINC150 answers agree, and the 7 that move
are near-ties. `RSIJEV_PAD_MULTIPLE=0` turns it off; packages without the setting, every earlier
release included, serve as before.

Inputs up to 32,768 tokens, nothing cut (longer gets a 422); up to 5,120 options; own-token
option pooling, set in `meta.json`.

The API returns a probability *and* a `confidence`, and they are different numbers: `confidence`
is the peak statistic Jev documents, `(K * p_max - 1) / (K - 1)`. Route on the probability of the
option you intend to act on. The response also carries `usage.depth`, the layer each question was
answered at.

## 6. Limitations

- The trunk was chosen from 9 full Decision Index 0.3 reads on the same benchmark it reports.
  The top five candidates read within 0.1 of each other, so the chosen one's lead is not
  separated from noise, and its score carries some selection on that benchmark. The package's
  own read (65.46) is a fresh run, after the choice.
- Calibration is worse than v6.1-VL 4B's: final ECE 0.064 against 0.048, 0.069 against 0.055
  with `auto` (§4.4).
- Images use the base's vision tower with no image training of our own. The 27B was not read on
  image tasks beyond eval_vision_v1.
- It needs one 96 GB GPU in bf16 (84.3 GB peak at the 32,768-token cap). There is no smaller
  build of it.
- Serve it as packaged: the numbers are defined on the padded path. The unpadded path
  (`RSIJEV_PAD_MULTIPLE=0`) moves about 0.4% of answers, all near-ties.
- `low` and `medium` were not run on the Decision Index; the RTX PRO 6000 was timed at the
  default effort only.
- The default's single threshold for multi-question requests, 0.8, was not chosen by a rule of
  its own; the Decision Index default run is its read.
- One seed per stage. The training corpora are not public.

## 7. How it got here

The 27B line trains with LoRA from a post-trained base, on the Decision Index training pools with
replay, then averages two fine-tunes as v6.1-VL 4B did. What this release established:

- **Averaging two fine-tunes beat both, at 27B too.** Member A alone read 64.83 on the 0.3
  index, member B 65.30, their average 65.59, the same pattern as v6.1-VL 4B.
- **The averages have plateaued.** Six averages of the same family read 65.37 to 65.59; uneven
  weights and a third ingredient did not move the score. A further fine-tune of member B read
  65.01 alone, +2.4 on tools and −3.0 on language against the average.
- **Exit heads average too.** The average's heads at layers 48 and 56 are the mean of the
  members' heads, never trained on the averaged trunk, and they beat either member at both
  layers.
- **Padding is what makes it fast at one request.** Short requests on the 27B are bound by
  kernel launches; padding to a fixed width lets CUDA graphs replay them (§5).

### Rerunning it

The 27B training stages (LoRA on four GPUs) and their corpora are not in this repository. Serving
the released model needs only the public code.
